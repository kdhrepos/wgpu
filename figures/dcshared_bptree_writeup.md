# DC-Shared on B+Tree — Evaluation Paragraph (Letter)

On b+tree (6×6 mesh, 64 M keys, 3 M queries), DC-Shared is **1.36× slower** than the online-classifier DC baseline despite being a zero-overhead idealization of any realizable classifier sync. The cost is not from chain traffic — DC-Shared actually issues 12× *fewer* chain events per L2 miss (1.8 vs 21) at 2× higher first-probe hit rate. It sits entirely on the request-side network: at every hop distance, request-flight latency is 1.4–2.9× higher on DC-Shared (e.g., 1-hop req: **614 ns vs 216 ns**), while response-flight latency is identical (46 vs 44 ns). Mechanism: DC-Shared's oracle-consistent view lifts the dominant PC (42 % of L2 traffic) from **Warm** to **Hot**, which in the protocol maps a 2×3 tiling (6 LH nodes, mostly forwarders at 23 % hit) to a 2×2 tiling (9 LH nodes at 45 % hit). Higher LH hit rate means the LH must become a *producing responder* (L2 pipeline read + data flit + cluster distribution) instead of a cheap forwarder. That serialization saturates the LH's request-input port, backing up source-side injection queues across the mesh by ~400 ns per hop. The tier abstraction thus fails a monotonicity requirement: refining classification improves the metric the DC protocol was designed to optimize (chain efficiency) but simultaneously activates a heavier per-LH role, and for a workload whose hot set is dominated by a handful of PCs, the latter dominates end-to-end latency.
